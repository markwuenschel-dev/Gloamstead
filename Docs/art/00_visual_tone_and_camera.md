# Visual Tone and Camera Direction

_Dark fantasy restoration with simple third-person play._

## Visual Target

Gloamstead should feel like realistic UE5 dark fantasy with enough stylized readability to support interpretation, silhouette reading, and supernatural clarity.

The world is bleak, cold, and damaged. The sanctuary/light is warm, fragile, and meaningful.

## Tone

Not cozy-first. Not horror-first. Not grimdark misery.

The target is:

> Bleak world, small light, fragile hope, beautiful dread.

## Day Look

Day should be quiet and uneasy rather than cheerful. The world can be beautiful, but it should feel wounded.

Visual motifs:

- ash-gray ground
- dead grasses
- broken stone
- pale fog
- cold sunlight
- old roots
- ruined shrines
- faint restored glow

## Night Look

Night should be threatening but readable. Darkness should be beautiful and eerie, not just black monster fog.

Visual motifs:

- cold blue/gray darkness
- subtle cyan/teal light from restored objects
- soft but readable silhouettes
- creeping corruption
- faint reflections
- distortions in fog
- dangerous beauty

## Camera

The game uses third-person camera as the primary perspective.

Combat and interaction complexity should remain simple. The camera should support:

- walking through the sanctuary
- reading environmental clues
- restoring objects
- observing night changes
- simple combat/cleansing
- emotional attachment to place

Avoid pulling the camera into full overhead management. Temporary focus/inspection views may exist, but the primary identity is third-person.

## Readability

Because interpretation is a core skill, visuals must communicate rules fairly.

Use:

- clear lighting states
- reactive object animations
- readable corruption spread
- consistent visual language for safe/unsafe spaces
- strong silhouettes
- restrained effects that do not obscure clues
