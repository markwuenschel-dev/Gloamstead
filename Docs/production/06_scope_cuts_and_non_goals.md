# Scope Cuts and Non-Goals

_Systems intentionally removed from the current direction._

## Purpose

This document protects the current direction from drifting back into crowded genres.

## Explicit Non-Goals

### Tower Defense

No tower-defense lane game. Restored structures can help with threats, but the player is not building trap lanes or optimizing DPS corridors.

### Horde Survival

Generic hordes are not the main loop. A direct assault can exist as a rare or tutorial consequence.

### Village Management

No colony management, villager schedules, morale simulation, job assignment system, or population economy in the core direction.

### Survival Crafting

No wood/stone/ore grind as the core loop. No hunger/thirst survival. No broad crafting treadmill.

### RTS/Colony Sim

No overhead command layer, squad control, worker micro, or economy expansion as core.

### Hack-and-Slash

Combat should be simple and pressure-focused, not a deep action system.

### Open World Sprawl

The game may have some open exploration, but should not begin as a large open-world survival project.

## If These Return Later

Any removed system must justify itself by strengthening the core sentence:

> I understood the warning. I restored the right place. I built the right thing. I survived because I learned the world.

If it does not strengthen that sentence, it probably belongs outside the game.


## Art Direction Non-Goals

### Pure Photorealism

Do not chase maximum realism as the primary identity. Pure photorealism risks making the game feel like survival horror, generic dark fantasy, or a UE5 environment showcase rather than a symbolic restoration game.

### Generic Dark Fantasy Grayness

Avoid endless gray ruins, fog, rubble, dead trees, and high-detail surfaces with no visual hierarchy. Gloamstead needs a visual grammar, not just mood.

### Cozy Fantasy Warmth

Restoration can be warm and beautiful, but the baseline tone is not cozy. Warmth should be earned, fragile, and sacred.

### Overdesigned Metaphor

Avoid making every surface too symbolically literal. Faded tapestry, mineral fracture, and luminous fantasy elements can be accents, but the world still needs grounded believability.

### VFX Noise

Particles, bloom, tendrils, and magical effects should not overwhelm environmental clues. VFX should behave like matter becoming uncanny, not generic spell effects.

## Art Direction Test

An art choice belongs if it strengthens at least one of these:

- the player can read the warning more fairly
- restoration feels meaningful and place-specific
- the sanctuary feels warmer without becoming cozy
- night consequences become more legible
- the Veil Heart feels alive, wounded, and miraculous
- the world feels bleak but recoverable

An art choice is suspect if it primarily adds realism, spectacle, density, or prettiness without supporting interpretation, restoration, or consequence.


## MVP Scope Direction

The current first playable target is approximately **2-6 hours**.

The first MVP should be one contained island/place rather than a broad open world. It can be semi-open, but should remain scaled down enough that the team can polish:

- the core loop
- fixed-location restoration
- dusk preparation
- a few distinct night consequences
- dawn payoff
- the Heart's presence and progression
- readable art direction

## Endings Scope

Multiple endings are desirable for the larger game because the player may decide how to grow, protect, exploit, restrain, or transform the Heart/light.

For MVP, full multiple-ending implementation is not required unless it directly strengthens the prototype's core loop. A better MVP target may be:

- one complete ending
- one altered ending hint
- one final decision seed
- or visible variables that could support endings later

Do not let ending branching pull scope away from proving the loop.
