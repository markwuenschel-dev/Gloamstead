# Gameplay Loop

_How a session of the current version should play._

For the clearest version of the loop, see `00_core_loop.md`. This file expands that loop into phases.

## Core Loop

1. Explore a ruined, bleak area in third-person.
2. Receive or discover a cryptic warning from the Veil Heart.
3. Study the environment for context clues.
4. Restore a meaningful structure or place.
5. Prepare for night based on interpretation.
6. Night tests the rule behind the warning.
7. Survive through understanding, simple combat, cleansing, activation, or emergency restoration.
8. Dawn provides feedback, healing, progression, and a clearer understanding of the world.

## Day Phase

Day is quiet, uneasy, and exploratory. The player walks through the ruined space, examines objects, restores structures, listens to the Veil Heart, and decides what kind of sanctuary they are creating.

Day activities may include:

- exploring nearby ruins
- examining environmental clues
- restoring lanterns, shrines, gardens, mirrors, roots, bells, or other ritual structures
- activating or arranging restored structures
- interpreting the Heart's fragments
- preparing for the next night
- learning how prior choices changed the world

## Dusk Phase

Dusk is the interpretive preparation window. The Heart may whisper a warning. The environment may change subtly. Certain restored pieces may react.

The player should feel:

> I have enough information to make a guess, but not enough to feel completely safe.

## Night Phase

Night is consequence under pressure.

Possible night pressures:

- corruption spreads through neglected structures
- a restored object is targeted for retrieval
- a structure becomes possessed from inside
- hidden enemies appear only under certain conditions
- paths vanish unless properly remembered by light
- a bargain or temptation appears
- a rare direct assault tests all preparations

Simple combat may happen, but night is not primarily about killing enemies.

## Dawn Phase

Dawn is the release, feedback, and payoff phase. It should show what the player learned, what changed, and what the night cost or rewarded.

Dawn may include:

- corruption receding
- restored structures blooming or failing
- the Heart becoming clearer
- new environmental access
- altered future warnings
- visible consequences of misunderstanding
- subtle world-healing payoff
- a night-earned resource reward
- visible scars from misunderstanding
- a clearer rule, hint, or memory echo

## Example Session

The Heart whispers: "Cold comes where no flame remembers."

The player explores and finds dead lantern posts around an old path. They restore one near the sanctuary.

At night, fog rolls in. The restored lantern creates a safe area and weakens small enemies that enter its light. The player learns that restored light defines temporary safety and that warnings correspond to physical places.

At dawn, the Heart's glow steadies. The world feels slightly less dead. The player now understands a basic rule.


## Visual Loop

The visual loop should mirror the mechanical loop.

1. **Explore:** the world feels cold, weathered, misted, and partially illegible.
2. **Warning:** the Veil Heart's fragment is echoed by small environmental signals: a light direction, a reacting object, a color shift, a sound source, a silhouette, or a material change.
3. **Study:** important clues have stronger value contrast and cleaner silhouettes than background decay.
4. **Restore:** the object or place regains warmth, clarity, and symbolic presence.
5. **Prepare:** restored structures visibly imply what they may protect, reveal, invite, or endanger.
6. **Night:** the world tests the player's interpretation through a distinct visual consequence language.
7. **Survive:** simple combat, cleansing, activation, or emergency restoration should read clearly through fog and darkness.
8. **Dawn:** successful understanding restores coherence: fog thins, colors warm, edges clarify, and the sanctuary breathes.

## Time-of-Day Art Notes

### Day

Quiet, uneasy, and cold. Detail should be grounded, but not noisy. The player should be able to identify meaningful structures from their silhouettes and placement.

### Dusk

Interpretive tension. The world should feel like it is preparing to answer the player's choices. Restored objects may pulse, dim, chime, cast longer shadows, or reveal subtle edge glows.

### Night

Night is not just darker day. Each night type should have an authored atmospheric behavior: crawling corruption, swallowed paths, mirrored silhouettes, dead silence, false warmth, fractured space, or rare siege violence.

### Dawn

Dawn is release and feedback. It should show what the player learned. The best dawns make the player feel that a small part of the world became more real again.


## First Night Teaching Target

The first night should teach the minimum grammar of the game:

> I did something during the day. I prepared at dusk. Night showed the result of my action.

It does not need to fully explain that nights can adapt to what the player did or did not do. The safer tutorial path is to teach clear cause and effect first, then let deeper adaptation emerge over later nights.

## Adaptation Introduction Guidance

Night adaptation should be discoverable before it becomes explicit.

Recommended sequence:

1. **Night 1:** clear action -> preparation -> result.
2. **Night 2 or 3:** a neglected clue or wrong restoration makes the night harder in a readable way.
3. **Later:** the player realizes the darkness is responding to patterns of restoration, neglect, alteration, and misunderstanding.

This preserves mystery without making the first night overloaded.
