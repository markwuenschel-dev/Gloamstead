# Core Game Pillars

_Design pillars for the current direction._

## Pillar 1: The Veil Heart Speaks, But Does Not Explain

The Veil Heart is not a quest marker, tutorial bot, or normal NPC. It gives poetic, esoteric, incomplete, and sometimes seemingly contradictory fragments.

The player gradually learns that these fragments are not flavor. They are clues to the world's rules.

## Pillar 2: Restoration Is The Main Action

The player restores meaningful structures and places. Restoration is not generic construction.

A lantern is not just a light source. It may remember paths, stabilize safe space, or reveal what the fog hides.

A garden is not just decoration. It may stabilize reality, slow corruption, or help the Heart grow.

A mirror is not just a reveal tool. It may expose hidden corruption or reflect dangers the player created.

## Pillar 3: Night Is Consequence

Night is not the automatic horde phase. Night is when the world responds.

A night may contain enemies, but enemies are not always the point. The point is the specific consequence being tested: corruption, possession, retrieval, silence, omen, fracture, or rare direct assault.

## Pillar 4: Combat Is Simple Pressure

Combat should be closer to simple Elder Scrolls-level input complexity than a modern action game. The player may move, jump, attack, block/ward/cleanse, and interact.

Combat exists to create urgency around the puzzle, not to become the main skill test.

## Pillar 5: The World Is Bleak But Recoverable

The world should feel dying, abandoned, cold, and half-forgotten. The player's actions bring warmth and light back into specific places.

The emotional tone is not cheerful cozy fantasy. It is fragile hope in a bleak world.

## Pillar 6: Hordes Are Rare, Not Default

A direct assault can exist as a special consequence or tutorial beat, but standardized horde defense is not the central structure.

## Pillar 7: Mystery Must Be Fair

The Heart's guidance can be cryptic, but it must not be arbitrary. The player needs environmental clues, repeated patterns, visible cause/effect, and enough feedback to learn.

Cryptic is good. Random-feeling punishment is bad.


## Art Direction Corollary

The art direction exists to reinforce the pillars, not to chase realism for its own sake.

- The Veil Heart speaks through fragments, so the environment must provide readable visual echoes of those fragments.
- Restoration is the main action, so restored places must visibly regain warmth, coherence, and symbolic function.
- Night is consequence, so each night type should have a distinct visual pressure language rather than defaulting to hordes in darkness.
- Combat is simple pressure, so threat readability matters more than animation complexity or visual noise.
- The world is bleak but recoverable, so baseline environments should be cold, withered, and half-forgotten while restored zones become sacred pockets of warmth.
- Mystery must be fair, so fog, darkness, stylization, and post-processing cannot obscure the clues the player needs.

Recommended visual identity:

> **Withered Gothic Realism** — painterly, romantic dark-fantasy realism with liminal memory atmospherics and ritualized restoration.
