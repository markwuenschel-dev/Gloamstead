# Player Experience

_What the player should feel and do._

## Primary Fantasy

The player is not a commander, mayor, survival crafter, or horde-killer. The player is a restorer and interpreter.

They are alone or nearly alone in a dying place, protecting a fragile source of light that may be ancient, wounded, confused, divine, natural, or something else entirely.

## Desired Feelings

- unease
- curiosity
- fragile hope
- personal attachment
- dread without horror overload
- relief at dawn
- pride in understanding
- responsibility toward the light

## What The Player Is Proud Of

After a good session, the player should think:

- I figured out what the warning meant.
- I restored the right structure.
- I survived because I paid attention.
- My sanctuary feels more alive.
- The world makes a little more sense now.

## Player Skill

The most important skill is interpretation.

Supporting skills:

- spatial awareness
- simple combat timing
- clue reading
- risk assessment
- memory of prior nights
- understanding symbolic building effects
- choosing when to restore, cleanse, or leave something alone

## Player Failure

The player should usually fail because they misunderstood, overreached, ignored clues, or chose a risky restoration without preparing for its consequence.

Failure should rarely feel like the player simply lacked DPS or grinding time.
